1. OoD-Bench: Quantifying and Understanding Two Dimensions of Out-of-Distribution Generalization
1. Paper
OoD-Bench: Quantifying and Understanding Two Dimensions of Out-of-Distribution Generalization — CVPR 2022. 

2. ID of the paper
CVPR 2022
Pages: 7947–7958.

3. What is the paper about and what did they discuss/propose?
The paper studies what happens when the training and testing distributions are different.

It identifies two types of distribution shift:

Diversity shift
Correlation shift
It creates a benchmark to measure these shifts and evaluates existing ML algorithms under them. 

4. GitHub repo
OoD-Bench GitHub

The repository provides the benchmark and modified DomainBed implementation. 

5. Future scopes and limitations
Potential directions:

Better adaptation to unseen distributions
Detect the type of distribution shift automatically
Develop models that adapt differently to diversity vs correlation shift
Test on more real-world datasets
Reduce performance degradation under severe shift
6. Feasibility of implementing that scope and publishing it
9/10

A strong project could be:

Adaptive ML for Unknown Distribution Shifts

Instead of using one fixed model:

Input data
   ↓
Detect distribution shift
   ↓
Identify shift type
   ↓
Select/adapt ML strategy
   ↓
Prediction
This is experimentally measurable and does not require training huge models.

7. Have those limitations/future scopes been overcome?
Partially — important.

There has been significant progress after this paper. In fact, the authors themselves released OoD-Bench+, published in TPAMI 2025, with improved shift quantification and additional datasets. 

So:

Original benchmark limitation → substantially addressed.

But:

Automatically adapting to an unknown shift → still a research opportunity.

Research potential: 9/10
Feasibility: 9/10 ⭐

Fishr tries to make models generalize better when the test distribution differs from training distributions.

The authors report that Fishr improves performance on the DomainBed benchmark and performs better than standard ERM in their experiments.

Therefore, "improve Fishr" alone isn't enough.

The stronger gap would be something specific such as:

1. Safetywashing: Do AI Safety Benchmarks Actually Measure Safety Progress?
1. Paper
Safetywashing: Do AI Safety Benchmarks Actually Measure Safety Progress? — NeurIPS 2024. 

2. ID of the paper
arXiv: 2407.21792
DOI: 10.52202/079017-2190 

3. What is the paper about and what did they discuss/propose?
The paper asks a very important question:

Do AI safety benchmarks actually measure safety, or are they simply measuring how capable a model is?

They analyzed many safety benchmarks and compared them with general model capabilities.

They found that many "safety" benchmarks are strongly correlated with model capability and scale. So a model can appear safer simply because it became more capable.

They call this problem "safetywashing."

4. GitHub repo
GitHub — Safetywashing 

5. Future scopes and limitations

The paper's main limitation is that the analysis depends on the existing safety benchmarks.

They suggest:

creating safety benchmarks less correlated with general capabilities
reporting correlation with model capabilities for new safety benchmarks
finding benchmarks that measure a genuinely separate safety property
studying safety areas not covered by their analysis 
6. Feasibility of implementing that scope + publishing?

Very high — 9/10

You don't need to train an LLM.

Possible project:

"Capability-Independent Evaluation of LLM Safety Benchmarks"

You could:

select 10–20 safety benchmarks
evaluate several open-source LLMs
measure capability vs safety correlation
introduce new evaluation dimensions
design a decorrelated safety benchmark
compare it with existing benchmarks
This is particularly suitable if you want a research-oriented project rather than a normal application.

7. Have those limitations/future scopes already been overcome?

Partially.

There has been substantial work on better safety evaluation since 2024, but the fundamental problem identified by Safetywashing is not completely solved.

The paper itself argues that many existing benchmarks remain entangled with general capability.
